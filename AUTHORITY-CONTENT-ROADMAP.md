# 90-Day Authority Content Roadmap

Recommended publishing plan for Wendyle Seno. These are proposed topics, not completed work or claims of experience. Each article should be based on a real, first-hand support case or verified project evidence, with customer and company details anonymized where required.

## Editorial principles

- Publish only work Wendyle can personally explain and defend in an interview.
- Remove merchant names, store URLs, customer data, tokens, credentials, ticket IDs, internal links, and confidential product details.
- Separate observed facts from hypotheses and clearly label limitations.
- Show the troubleshooting path: symptoms, context, reproduction, tests, root cause, resolution or workaround, escalation evidence, and reusable lesson.
- Use screenshots or code only when publication is permitted and sensitive values are removed.
- Prefer one useful original article every one to two weeks over high-volume generic content.

## Days 1–30: Establish troubleshooting credibility

### 1. Shopify theme debugging: separating app behavior from theme behavior

Explain a repeatable browser-based process for comparing themes, checking app embeds, inspecting the DOM, and isolating CSS or JavaScript conflicts. Use a real anonymized example only after confirming it is safe to publish.

### 2. A practical API troubleshooting checklist for SaaS support

Cover request method, endpoint, authentication state, headers, payload, status code, response body, environment, rate limits, timestamps, and correlation IDs. Avoid publishing real keys, tokens, or private endpoints.

### 3. Writing an engineering escalation that can be reproduced

Show the structure of a strong escalation: customer impact, expected and actual results, exact steps, environment, evidence, scope, workaround, and remaining questions.

## Days 31–60: Demonstrate front-end and support depth

### 4. Diagnosing a Shopify widget that appears on the wrong variant

Use the preorder case study as the starting point. Explain state, selected-variant checks, campaign data, and how support can distinguish configuration from implementation issues without inventing business results.

### 5. CSS customization without breaking responsive storefront behavior

Explain how to inspect existing styles, limit selector scope, test breakpoints, avoid layout shift, and preserve merchant theme behavior.

### 6. Turning repeated tickets into a maintainable support playbook

Use the support-dashboard project as evidence. Describe how recurring causes, checklists, snippets, response templates, and escalation requirements can be organized without exposing internal documentation.

## Days 61–90: Show ownership and cross-team value

### 7. From customer report to root cause: an anonymized support investigation

Select one real case that is safe to publish. Walk through the evidence and decision points, including what was ruled out and why.

### 8. Customer workaround versus engineering fix

Explain how to communicate a safe short-term workaround while preserving the evidence and scope needed for a durable product fix.

### 9. Measuring support documentation quality without vanity metrics

Discuss practical indicators such as findability, completeness, reproduction success, reduced missing evidence, and clearer handoffs. Use no numerical improvement claims unless Wendyle has source data.

## Reusable article outline

1. Problem or symptom
2. Why the issue was difficult
3. Verified environment and constraints
4. Reproduction steps
5. Investigation and tests
6. Root cause or current best-supported diagnosis
7. Resolution, workaround, or escalation
8. Customer communication considerations
9. Documentation or prevention lesson
10. Limitations and what would be checked next

## Topics to avoid

- Generic “top tips” articles that contain no first-hand insight
- Fabricated customer stories, metrics, outcomes, or quotes
- Confidential internal procedures copied verbatim
- Pages created only to repeat keyword variations
- Claims that a technique guarantees rankings, indexing, AI citations, or hiring outcomes
